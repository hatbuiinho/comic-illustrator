# Page 15 — fresh storyboard options v1

Frame: `page15-chapter1-ending-hero`, ratio `2.954:1`.

Shared thesis: at blue hour, Nalini pauses before redrawing and for the first
time sees the immense unfinished monastery as future lived space. His body and
gaze must share one believable direction into the site. The site is an open
lowland plain with no mountains. Leaf shelters are lightweight temporary
canopies, not pavilions.

## Option 1 — `page15-option1-vast-site-profile`

- Focus: Nalini's quiet realization against the scale of responsibility.
- Moment: he has lowered the obsolete rolls and looks across the site.
- Camera: medium profile from his front-left side; enough torso is visible to
  prove that chest, shoulders, head and gaze all face into the worksite.
- Visible count: one — Nalini.
- Placement: Nalini occupies the right quarter; the left three quarters open
  into long foundation rows, repeated stakes and two small low leaf canopies.
- Negative space: a broad blue-hour interval between his face and the nearest
  warm torch.
- Crop: lower torso may leave the bottom edge between chest and waist; hands and
  plans are optional support and need not be complete.
- Composition strategy: `phi-grid` primary; `negative-space` support.
- Strength: clearest facial emotion plus unmistakable vast-site scale.
- Risk: must prevent the site from overpowering Nalini at layout size.

## Option 2 — `page15-option2-torchline-ots`

- Focus: the worksite as Nalini now sees it.
- Moment: a still breath after the carriage has disappeared.
- Camera: medium over-the-shoulder from slightly behind and to his right; one eye
  and cheek remain readable in three-quarter profile.
- Visible count: one — Nalini.
- Placement: Nalini forms a left foreground mass; long low foundation lines lead
  across the spine toward a staggered line of humble torch-lit canopies in the
  right mid-distance. Flat fields continue beyond the frame.
- Negative space: dark worked earth and open air between Nalini and the first
  shelter; no scenic landmark fills it.
- Crop: shoulder/torso may be cut decisively by left and bottom edges; no joint
  crop. Plans may remain entirely out of frame.
- Composition strategy: `leading-lines` primary; `negative-space` support.
- Strength: strongest subjective/cinematic sense of seeing through Nalini.
- Risk: facial acting is subtler and must not collapse into a back view.

## Option 3 — `page15-option3-foundation-threshold`

- Focus: Nalini stands between the obsolete plan and the future lived place.
- Moment: he pauses at the edge of a long foundation trench, without drawing.
- Camera: low eye-level medium-wide across the trench; not a heroic low angle.
- Visible count: one — Nalini.
- Placement: a foundation edge begins in the lower-left foreground and recedes
  past the spine; Nalini stands right of center in three-quarter profile facing
  left into the site. A partial thin-pole leaf canopy sits far left, cropped by
  the frame so it reads as part of a larger worksite rather than a featured hut.
- Negative space: open flat field and repeated stakes behind the eye line.
- Crop: Nalini remains a natural unified figure from head through upper legs;
  plans rest low against the body and may be partly hidden.
- Composition strategy: `leading-lines` primary; `Hitchcock's Rule` support.
- Strength: best balance between human scale and an immense unfinished site.
- Risk: lower camera must remain reflective, not monumental.

## Recommendation

Option 1 is the strongest chapter-ending balance: readable emotion, coherent
pose and a worksite that feels much larger than Nalini. Option 2 is more
subjective and cinematic; Option 3 carries the strongest builder/site metaphor.
